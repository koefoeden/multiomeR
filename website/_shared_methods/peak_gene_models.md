GEX and ATAC values are residualized against donor, scaled log RNA and ATAC depth and the class's accessibility components described below; the Pearson correlation of the residuals is reported as a descriptive summary. The hierarchical analysis fits donor fixed intercepts, depth covariates, the accessibility components and a donor-varying slope for the within-donor-centered peak value by profiled restricted maximum likelihood, with Kenward–Roger inference for the average slope [@kenward1997_kenward_roger]. A peak is tested only when its accessibility varies within at least eight donors; with fewer, the random-slope test has too few degrees of freedom to be informative, so its pairs remain untested. Fits failing numerical diagnostics retain their estimates but no inferential P-value. Peaks and genes that both vary along shared chromatin states correlate whether or not they interact. The accessibility components remove the leading states: the top ten principal components, or one per ten residual degrees of freedom when fewer, of the class's donor- and depth-residualized, standardized log1p CPM of all candidate peaks accessible in at least 5% of its pseudobulks across chromosomes. Genes that vary along the remaining states still correlate with many peaks, so, like Signac's comparison of each peak–gene correlation with matched background peaks [@stuart2021_signac], each candidate is compared with its own gene: up to 200 of the branch's candidate peaks more than 10 Mb from the gene's TSS are scored with the same model. The candidate's signed normal score, the normal quantile of its P-value signed by the slope, is standardized by the mean and SD of the gene's background scores, given at least ten of them, and rescaled by the class's background SD within its peak-detection quintile. A candidate's empirical P-value is the share of the class's rescaled background scores at least as extreme, and these are Benjamini–Hochberg-corrected within each annotation class over the complete eligible pair family, counting untested and unreliable pairs. A candidate link requires an estimable positive average slope above the gene's background mean and an FDR below 0.05, excluding self-promoter peaks. SuSiE fine-mapping, with up to ten single effects and 95% credible sets whose peaks correlate with a minimum absolute correlation of at least 0.5 (purity), prioritizes peaks on the same residualized values for genes with at least one candidate link, up to the 50 genes with the lowest FDR per class and chromosome and the 500 peaks per gene with the largest absolute residual correlation [@wang2020_susie]. These model-based associations do not establish causal enhancer–gene regulation, and background peaks more than 10 Mb away cannot separate a specific enhancer from covariation within a local chromatin domain.

Top-link plots rank estimable positive hierarchical associations by their uncalibrated Kenward–Roger P-value, without a significance cutoff, excluding self-promoter peaks; gene-body peaks remain eligible. Each plot shows the gene context, the focal class's insertion coverage and the donor-residual scatter.
