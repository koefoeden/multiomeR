The optional peak–gene correlation module operates separately within assigned WNN annotation classes of at least 200 nuclei and requires donor identifiers and per-nucleus GEX and ATAC depth metadata; classes lacking them are skipped with a diagnostic. Consensus ATAC peaks are paired with gene transcription start sites (TSSs) on the same chromosome within 250 kb of the peak center, and each pair is classified as self-promoter (−1,500 to +500 bp around the TSS), gene-body, proximal (within 10 kb) or distal. Within each class, nuclei of donors contributing at least 40 nuclei are partitioned into mutually exclusive ATAC-state bins by *k*-means clustering of scaled ATAC LSI dimensions 2–20, Harmony-corrected where configured; the number of bins is the median number of nuclei per donor divided by 20, rounded down and capped at 20. Counts are summed within each donor–state combination to create non-overlapping pseudobulks; pseudobulks of fewer than 20 nuclei are dropped, and donors and states are pruned iteratively until every donor contributes at least two states.

GEX and ATAC pseudobulk counts are separately scaled to counts per million, using the metadata-derived pseudobulk depth, and log1p-transformed. Genes and peaks must be detected in at least 5% of pseudobulks, and a chromosome branch requires at least 10 pseudobulks and 5 residual degrees of freedom. A measurement-support filter then removes hypotheses whose gene and peak are not both supported, with count thresholds scaled by each pseudobulk's depth relative to the class median but never below 2. The gene and the peak must each reach their thresholds in at least the larger of a minimum number and a fraction of the pseudobulks, and in at least two pseudobulks of each of a minimum number of shared donors. The lenient preset requires RNA and ATAC counts of at least 5 and 3, the larger of 6 pseudobulks and 10% of them, and two shared donors; the default moderate preset raises the counts to 10 and 5; and the strict preset also requires the larger of 10 pseudobulks and 20% of them, and three shared donors with three supporting pseudobulks each. Excluded hypotheses are never tested and do not enter the multiple-testing family.
