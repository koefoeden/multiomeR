Fragments on the standard chromosomes are used for peak calling. Peak-calling groups are defined by a configurable metadata column of the GEX-retained nuclei, by default their GEX clusters. Groups larger than 50,000 nuclei are randomly downsampled for peak discovery. Each group's discovery nuclei are split into two random halves. Peaks are called in each half with either MACS3 or the BPCells tile-based caller [@zhang2008_macs; @parks2025_bpcells]. MACS3 runs on exported group fragments with a −75-bp shift, a 150-bp extension and summit calling. The tile-based caller runs on the stored fragments, using 500-bp tiles and an FDR of 0.01. MACS3 reads each exported fragment as a single read at its left end, so only one of its two insertions enters the pileup. Consensus peaks follow the ArchR strategy of iterative fixed-width peak construction [@granja2021_archr]. Summits are extended by 250 bp to 500-bp peaks, and peaks overlapping any blacklisted region of the reference genome are removed. Overlapping peaks are collapsed by summit significance, first within each half and then across the two halves. Only peaks called in both halves are kept. Each group keeps its strongest peaks, up to 500 per discovery nucleus and 150,000 in all. The group-level sets are then combined into one non-overlapping consensus set. Here, overlapping peaks are ranked by the quantile of their summit significance within their group. BPCells counts fragment insertions (by default) or overlaps within these peaks for the GEX-retained nuclei.

ATAC dimensional reduction starts from the consensus peak matrix of the nuclei that pass the aggregation-level ATAC exclusions. Signac's TF-IDF method 1, with a scale factor of 10,000, normalizes this matrix [@stuart2021_signac]. A BPCells singular-value decomposition of the result gives the latent semantic indexing (LSI) embeddings [@parks2025_bpcells]. The pipeline keeps all LSI components and does not test them for correlation with sequencing depth. Instead, the default dimension settings leave out the first component, which often reflects depth.
